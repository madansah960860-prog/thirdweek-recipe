Quick weeknight dinners

Seven dinners that go from a cold kitchen to the table in forty minutes or less, chopping counted. No pre-cooked shortcuts, and no assumption that you prepped on Sunday.

The recipes in this collection have one thing in common: nothing in them takes longer than the longest step. That sounds obvious and it is the thing most weeknight recipes get wrong. A recipe with a 30-minute braise and a 5-minute salad is a 35-minute recipe, not a 30-minute one, because you cannot chop while you stir.

We time these from walking into the kitchen, and we include the time it takes to find the can opener. Where a recipe genuinely needs a rest or a chill, it is in the total time and flagged in the title.

Two of the seven have their own full pages with the servings scaler and printable card. The other five are published in full below, with the same ingredient checklists and the same detail.

Tuna and White Bean Salad with Lemon

Prep time
12 minutes
Cook time
None
Total time
12 minutes
Yield
2 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Fish. Contains no gluten as written.

The pantry dinner we fall back on more than any other. It needs no heat, it improves while it sits, and every component keeps for months in a cupboard. Buy tuna packed in olive oil rather than water; the oil becomes part of the dressing and the fish is far less dry.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the salad

twoFor the dressing

Whisk the mustard in first. It is the emulsifier that keeps the dressing from separating on the plate.

Method

  1. Soak the onion

    Put the sliced red onion in a small bowl, cover with cold water and leave it for five minutes while you do everything else. This takes the harsh edge off without cooking it.

  2. Make the dressing

    Whisk the lemon juice, mustard, salt and pepper together, then pour in the olive oil and reserved tuna oil in a thin stream while whisking. It should thicken slightly.

  3. Combine

    Drain the onion and pat it dry. Toss the beans, celery, onion and parsley with two thirds of the dressing. Flake the tuna in large pieces over the top and fold once, gently. Large flakes are better than a mashed mixture.

  4. Rest, then serve

    Let the bowl sit for five minutes. Add the arugula and remaining dressing just before serving so it does not wilt.

Cook's tips

  • Buy tuna in olive oil, in glass if you can. Water-packed tuna is a different and much drier product, and you lose the dressing base.
  • Rinse the beans properly. Thirty seconds under cold running water removes the starchy canning liquid and about a third of the sodium.
  • Dress the beans, not the leaves. Beans absorb dressing and taste seasoned; leaves just get heavy. Add the arugula at the last moment.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 2 days refrigerated in an airtight container, without the arugula. It is a cold dish, so there is nothing to reheat. Do not freeze.

Garlic Butter Pork Chops with Smashed Potatoes

Prep time
10 minutes
Cook time
25 minutes
Total time
35 minutes
Yield
2 servings
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Milk. Contains no gluten as written.

A basted chop is one of the best returns on effort in home cooking. You sear one side hard, flip it, drop in butter, garlic and thyme, and spoon the foaming butter over the top for two minutes. That is the whole technique, and it turns a $6 chop into something worth sitting down for.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the potatoes

twoFor the chops

One-inch chops are the sweet spot. Thinner chops overcook before they color.

Method

  1. Start the potatoes

    Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Boil the potatoes in salted water for 15 minutes until a knife slides in easily. Drain, spread on a sheet pan, and press each one flat with the bottom of a glass. Toss with the olive oil and salt and roast 20 minutes until the edges crisp.

  2. Season and rest the chops

    Season the chops all over and leave them on the counter while the potatoes cook. A chop straight from the refrigerator cooks unevenly.

  3. Sear

    Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the chops away from you and do not move them for 4 minutes. Flip once.

  4. Baste

    Add the butter, garlic and thyme. Tilt the pan toward you and spoon the foaming butter over the chops continuously for 2 to 3 minutes, until an instant-read thermometer in the center reads 145°F (63°C).

  5. Rest

    Move the chops to a board and rest 5 minutes. Serve with the potatoes and the pan butter poured over.

Cook's tips

  • Pull at 145°F (63°C) and rest. USDA lowered the safe pork temperature to 145°F with a three-minute rest years ago. Cooking pork to 160°F is why a generation grew up thinking it was dry.
  • Leave the garlic skins on. Peeled cloves burn in butter within a minute. Crushed cloves in their skins perfume the butter and stay sweet.
  • Smash the potatoes while hot. Cooled potatoes crumble instead of flattening, and you lose the craggy surface that turns crisp.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 days refrigerated; USDA guidance for cooked pork is 3 to 4 days at 40°F (4°C) or below. Reheat sliced pork gently in a covered skillet with a splash of broth. Reheat potatoes at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes to bring the crunch back.

Crispy Chicken Cutlet Sandwiches

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
30 minutes
Yield
4 sandwiches
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Wheat, eggs, milk.

Pounding chicken breasts thin turns a 25-minute protein into a 6-minute one and gives you far more crisp surface per bite. The breading station is three shallow bowls and it is worth setting up properly, because a cutlet with patchy coating fries unevenly.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the cutlets

twoFor the sandwiches

Salt the tomato slices and leave them on a paper towel for five minutes. Wet tomato is what makes a sandwich collapse.

Method

  1. Set up the breading station

    Three shallow bowls: flour with half the salt and the pepper; beaten egg with water; panko mixed with Parmesan and the remaining salt. Keep one hand for dry and one for wet or you will bread your own fingers.

  2. Bread the cutlets

    Flour each cutlet and shake off the excess, dip in egg and let it drip, then press firmly into the panko on both sides. Set on a rack for 5 minutes so the coating sets.

  3. Fry

    Heat the oil in a wide skillet over medium-high until a breadcrumb sizzles immediately. Fry two cutlets at a time for 3 minutes per side until deep golden and cooked to 165°F (74°C). Drain on a rack, not paper towels.

  4. Make the lemon mayonnaise

    Stir the mayonnaise, lemon juice and grated garlic together.

  5. Build

    Spread both cut sides of each roll, add lettuce, then the hot cutlet, then the tomato on top. Lettuce underneath keeps the bottom of the roll from going soft.

Cook's tips

  • Rest the breaded cutlets for five minutes before frying. The coating hydrates and grips instead of sliding off in the pan.
  • Drain on a wire rack. Paper towels trap steam and the underside goes soft within a minute.
  • Pound between two sheets of parchment, not plastic wrap. Parchment does not tear, and you can use a heavy skillet if you do not own a mallet.

Storage and reheating. Cooked cutlets keep 3 days refrigerated per USDA guidance for cooked poultry. Reheat on a rack at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes. Do not microwave them; the coating turns to paste. Assemble sandwiches only when you are eating.

Cacio e Pepe with Toasted Pepper

Prep time
5 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
20 minutes
Yield
2 servings
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Wheat, milk.

Four ingredients, and all four of them are technique. The sauce is an emulsion of cheese, starch and water, and it breaks if the pan is too hot. Take the pan off the heat before the cheese goes anywhere near it. Everyone splits this sauce the first time; almost nobody splits it the third time.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the pasta

twoFor the sauce

Grate the cheese yourself on the fine side of a box grater. Pre-grated cheese carries anti-caking starch that turns the sauce grainy.

Method

  1. Toast the pepper

    Crack the peppercorns coarsely. Toast them in a dry skillet over medium heat for 60 seconds, moving constantly, until they smell sharp and floral. Set aside.

  2. Cook the pasta in less water than usual

    Use about 6 cups of water instead of a full pot. Less water means more concentrated starch, which is the backbone of the sauce. Cook one minute short of al dente.

  3. Make a paste

    Put the grated Pecorino in a bowl. Add about 3 tablespoons of the hot pasta water and stir hard into a thick, smooth paste. Getting the cheese wet before it meets the pan is the whole trick.

  4. Combine off the heat

    Drain the pasta, reserving a cup of water. Return the pasta to the warm, not hot, pan with the butter, toasted pepper and a splash of water. Take the pan off the burner entirely, add the cheese paste, and toss vigorously with tongs, adding water a tablespoon at a time until the sauce is glossy and coats each strand.

  5. Serve straight away

    This sauce waits for nobody. Warmed bowls, on the table, immediately.

Cook's tips

  • Off the heat, always. Pecorino seizes above roughly 175°F (80°C). If the pan is hot enough to sizzle, it is hot enough to break your sauce.
  • Use less water to cook the pasta. The starch concentration in the cooking water is what emulsifies the cheese. A big pot dilutes exactly what you need.
  • If it breaks, add more hot water and keep tossing. A split sauce can usually be brought back with more liquid and more agitation. Do not add more cheese; that makes it worse.

Storage and reheating. Best eaten immediately and does not store well; the sauce separates on cooling and does not come back. If you must, refrigerate 1 day and revive in a pan over low heat with plenty of water.

Sheet-Pan Salmon with Zucchini Ribbons

Prep time
10 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
25 minutes
Yield
2 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Fish. Contains no gluten or dairy as written.

High heat and a short window. Salmon at 425°F (220°C) cooks in about twelve minutes for a one-inch fillet, and the zucchini ribbons underneath collect the fat as it renders. It is a complete dinner from one pan with about four minutes of actual work.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the pan

twoTo finish

Method

  1. Heat the oven

    Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C), or 400°F (200°C) on convection. Line a sheet pan with parchment.

  2. Lay the zucchini bed

    Toss the zucchini ribbons with 2 tablespoons of the olive oil and half the salt, and spread them across the pan in a loose, uneven layer. Do not pack them down; you want air between the ribbons.

  3. Add the salmon

    Rub the fillets with the remaining oil and season with the rest of the salt and the pepper. Set them skin side down on the zucchini and tuck lemon slices around them.

  4. Roast

    Roast 12 to 15 minutes, until the salmon flakes at the thickest point and reaches 145°F (63°C). A one-inch fillet is usually done at 12.

  5. Finish

    Squeeze over the remaining lemon, scatter the dill and capers, and drizzle with the extra-virgin olive oil.

Cook's tips

  • Use a peeler for the zucchini, not a knife. Ribbons cook in the same window as the fish. Half-inch chunks do not; they will still be raw at the center.
  • Pat the salmon completely dry. Surface moisture steams the top of the fillet and stops it taking on color.
  • 145°F (63°C) is the USDA number, but 130°F to 135°F is where most cooks prefer salmon. If you cook it below the USDA temperature, know that you are making that choice, and do not serve it that way to anyone pregnant, very young, elderly or immunocompromised.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 days refrigerated per USDA guidance for cooked fish. Salmon is good cold, flaked into a salad the next day. If reheating, use a covered skillet over low heat for 3 minutes rather than a microwave.

What makes a weeknight recipe actually fast

The pattern held across every round of testing. Fast recipes are not the ones with the fewest ingredients. They are the ones where the slow step runs unattended while you do everything else.

  • One heat source, one long step. If the oven is doing 25 minutes of work, that is your window to make everything else.
  • Cut small. Half-inch cubes cook in half the time of one-inch cubes. Knife work is the cheapest speed you can buy.
  • Salt early, acid late. Salt needs time to travel. Lemon juice and vinegar go in at the end, off the heat, or they turn dull.
  • Boil the water first. Before you take your coat off. Twelve minutes of pot-watching is twelve minutes you never get back.
  • Buy the shortcut that is genuinely equivalent. Canned beans, jarred roasted peppers and frozen peas tested the same as the versions you make yourself. Pre-grated cheese and jarred garlic did not.

Our weekly meal plans sequence these so that one night's leftovers become the next night's shortcut.

What decides success in this category

Four things account for almost every weeknight test that ran long or came out flat.

  • A pan that is not hot yet. Food added early sticks, steams and adds ten minutes you did not budget for. Wait for the shimmer.
  • A crowded surface. Two half-sheet pans browned faster than one packed pan every time we ran it. Crowding turns roasting into steaming.
  • Prep started before the heat. If the oven is not preheating and the water is not on before you pick up a knife, the recipe is already behind.
  • No thermometer. Pork pulled at 145°F and chicken at 165°F is the line between juicy and ruined. Most of our failed rounds were eyeballed.
