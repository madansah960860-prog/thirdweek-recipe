Charred Black Bean and Sweet Potato Tacos

Half-inch cubes of sweet potato roasted at 425°F until the corners blacken, smoky black beans, and a crema blended while the pan is still in the oven.

Developed and retested in the Saltgrain test kitchen by Priya Raghunathan Published 06/11/2026 Last tested 08/30/2026
Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
30 minutes
Total time
45 minutes
Yield
8 tacos (4 servings)
Difficulty
Easy
Course
Dinner

Vegetarian Mexican & Tex-Mex Budget Allergens: milk (from the crema and cheese)

This is the cheapest dinner on the site. Two cans of beans, a pound and a half of sweet potatoes and a bunch of cilantro is most of the grocery cost, and it lands on the table in forty-five minutes with one sheet pan and one small skillet in the sink.

The word doing the work in the title is charred. Sweet potato carries enough sugar to caramelize, but only if the oven is hot and the pan is not crowded. At 425°F (220°C) in a single layer you get blackened corners and a soft center. At 375°F with the pan piled up you get orange mush. We cooked both, side by side, and the gap is not subtle.

Warm the tortillas directly over a gas flame or in a dry skillet, twenty seconds a side. A cold tortilla out of the bag tastes like the bag. A warmed one tastes like corn. It costs three minutes at the end and it is the step most people leave out.

How we tested this

Three rounds on an 18 by 13 inch half sheet pan. Round one cut the sweet potato into inch cubes and the centers were still steamy at 30 minutes, so round two dropped to half-inch. Round two also pushed a doubled batch onto one pan, and the vegetables came out pale and wet, which is why step two tells you to split across two pans instead. Round three moved the lime juice in the beans to the end of the simmer rather than the start, because lime added early cooks off and the beans read flat. The crema was blended three ways. A standard blender with the full 2 tablespoons of water was the only one that poured rather than sat in a lump.

Method

  1. Heat the oven and dress the vegetables

    Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C), or 400°F (200°C) on convection, with a rack in the upper third. Toss the sweet potato, red onion and bell pepper with the olive oil, chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, salt and pepper on a half sheet pan.

  2. Spread it properly

    Push everything into a single layer with gaps between the pieces. If your pan looks full, split it across two pans. This is the step that decides whether the vegetables char or steam.

  3. Roast

    Roast for 25 to 30 minutes, turning once at the 18-minute mark, until the sweet potato is tender through and blackened at the corners.

  4. Make the crema

    While the pan roasts, blend the sour cream, cilantro, lime juice, garlic, salt and water until smooth and pale green. If you do not have a blender, chop the cilantro and garlic very finely and stir them in; it will be flecked rather than smooth, which is fine.

  5. Cook the beans

    Heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium. Add the garlic and cumin and cook 30 seconds until fragrant. Add the drained beans, the chipotle and its adobo sauce, and ¼ cup water. Simmer for 6 to 8 minutes, mashing about a quarter of the beans against the side of the pan so the mixture holds together. Finish with the lime juice and salt.

  6. Warm the tortillas

    Char the tortillas one at a time directly over a low gas flame for about 20 seconds a side, turning with tongs, or toast them in a dry skillet over medium-high. Stack them in a clean kitchen towel to stay soft.

  7. Build

    Spread beans on each tortilla, pile on the roasted vegetables, add avocado, drizzle with the crema and scatter cilantro and queso fresco. Serve with lime wedges and eat them standing up, over the pan, like everyone actually does.

Food safety

Rinse canned beans under cold running water before use. Wash cilantro and all fresh produce under running water even when the bag says it is pre-washed, in line with FDA produce guidance. Refrigerate the crema promptly; dairy left at room temperature for more than two hours should be discarded.

Cook's tips

  • Cut the sweet potato smaller than feels right. Half-inch cubes roast in 25 minutes and give you the highest ratio of charred surface to soft interior. Inch cubes take 40 minutes and taste steamed in the middle.
  • Buy a can of chipotles in adobo and freeze the rest. Spoon the leftovers into an ice cube tray, freeze, and bag them. One cube is roughly one chili plus sauce, and the can will otherwise die in your refrigerator. A can runs about $2.50 at most Kroger stores.
  • Mash some of the beans, not all of them. A quarter mashed gives you a filling that holds on the tortilla without turning into refried paste. Whole beans roll off; fully mashed beans lose their texture.
  • Double the crema. It keeps five days, and it is excellent on eggs, roast potatoes and any grain bowl you build the next day.

What goes wrong

  • Orange mush instead of charred corners. The cubes were too big or the pan was too full. Half-inch cubes, one layer, gaps you can see between the pieces.
  • Tortillas that crack as you fold them. They went on cold. Char them 20 seconds a side and stack them in a clean towel so their own steam keeps them pliable.
  • Filling that slides straight off. Nothing was mashed. Crush about a quarter of the beans against the side of the pan so the mixture holds on the tortilla.

Substitutions

  • Vegan: swap the sour cream for unsweetened cashew cream or a plant-based sour cream and leave out the queso fresco. Everything else is already plant-based.
  • No chipotle: ½ teaspoon smoked paprika plus a pinch of cayenne gets you most of the way to the same smoke and heat.
  • Butternut squash works exactly like the sweet potato and roasts in the same window.
  • Pinto or kidney beans substitute directly for black beans.
  • Flour tortillas: use 8-inch flour tortillas and warm them the same way. The tacos will be softer and less structural.
  • No cilantro: if cilantro tastes like soap to you, use flat-leaf parsley and add an extra teaspoon of lime juice.

Make ahead, storage and reheating

  • Make ahead: the crema keeps 5 days refrigerated. The bean mixture can be made 3 days ahead. Cut the vegetables the night before and keep them covered.
  • Storage: store components separately in airtight containers, refrigerated within two hours. Assembled tacos go soggy in under an hour.
  • Freezing: the bean mixture freezes for 3 months. Roasted sweet potato comes back watery and soft, so freeze the beans and roast the vegetables fresh.
  • Reheating: spread the vegetables on a sheet pan and reheat at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes to bring the edges back. Warm the beans in a saucepan with a splash of water.

What you need to cook it

A half sheet pan, a small skillet, and a blender or food processor for the crema. Tongs make warming tortillas over a flame much less exciting. Our pantry guide covers the chili powders and canned goods worth keeping in.
