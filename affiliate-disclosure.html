Advertising & affiliate disclosure

How this site makes money, in full: display advertising, affiliate commissions, paid downloads, membership, sponsored recipes and newsletter sponsorship.

Last updated 09/14/2026. This page is written in plain English wherever we can manage it. If anything here is unclear, ask us and we will explain it and probably rewrite it.

The short version

This site makes money from advertising, from affiliate commissions on products we link to, from paid downloads and memberships, and occasionally from sponsored recipes. Every one of those is labeled where it appears. Nobody pays us for a positive opinion, and no sponsor gets to change a method or a verdict.

What this means in practice

Assume every product link on this site can earn Saltgrain a commission, and assume it costs you nothing extra. We buy and use the equipment before we link to it, and the cheaper pan wins when the cheaper pan performs, even though the cheaper pan pays us less. Display advertising is labeled. Sponsored work is labeled above the headline, before the first paragraph. Anything sent to us free is disclosed in the sentence where the product is first named. No payment has moved a temperature, a timing or a verdict, and if one tried, the answer would be no and the attempt would end up in the newsletter.

Advertising

Display advertisements appear on recipe, category and article pages. They are labeled as advertisements, they are visually distinct from our content, and they sit between sections or in the sidebar. They never appear above the recipe you came for, they never sit next to navigation or buttons, and there are none at all on our legal pages or our error page.

Ads are served by third-party vendors, which may include Google. Those vendors may use cookies to select and measure ads, but only if you have accepted advertising cookies through our banner. The cookie policy explains this and the privacy policy covers your opt-out rights.

We do not control which specific advertisement you see, and an advertisement appearing here is not an endorsement by us.

Affiliate links, and the FTC

Some links to products on this site are affiliate links. If you click one and buy something, we may receive a commission from the retailer. It costs you nothing extra. The price is the same as it would be if you had gone to the retailer directly.

The Federal Trade Commission requires that material connections between a publisher and a seller be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, close to the recommendation itself. The obligation is set out in the FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255. That is why you will see a disclosure box next to the link, not only on this page and not only in the footer.

Here is what that disclosure looks like in practice, and it appears on every page where we link to a product:

Affiliate link disclosure. The product links in this section are affiliate links. If you buy through one we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. We only list things we use, and a commission never buys a recommendation. How this works.

Our rules for affiliate links

  • We own it and use it. Nothing goes on the equipment page unless it is in our kitchen.
  • Commission rate never affects placement. We recommend the $18 sheet pan over the $60 one because it performs the same, and we say so, even though the commission is smaller.
  • We say when something is not worth buying. The equipment page has a list of things to skip, which is not a page that makes money and is one of the most useful things on the site.
  • We do not stuff links into old articles. Links appear where a product is genuinely relevant.
  • Prices are indicative. Retail prices in the United States change constantly. Treat every price on this site as a rough range, not a quote.

Saltgrain Kitchen Co. participates in retailer affiliate programs, and every program is joined in the company's own name. Where a program requires specific disclosure language, that language appears verbatim on the pages carrying its links as well as here.

Sponsored and brand-partnered recipes

Occasionally a brand pays us to develop a recipe using their product. When that happens:

  • The page carries a clear Sponsored recipe label at the very top, above the headline, before the first paragraph.
  • The label names the sponsor and states plainly that they paid for the recipe to be developed.
  • The sponsor may check factual claims about their own product. They do not approve, edit or veto our copy, our method or our conclusions.
  • If the recipe does not work with their product, we say so, and if we cannot make something we would actually cook we do not publish and we return the fee.

There are currently no sponsored recipes live on this site. When there are, they will be labeled exactly as described above.

Products sent to us

Companies sometimes send us equipment or ingredients to try. Receiving something free is a material connection under FTC guidance and we disclose it in the piece, in the sentence where the product is first mentioned. Being sent something does not oblige us to write about it, and most of what gets sent never appears here.

Paid products we sell ourselves

  • Downloadable cookbooks, $14 each in USD. Sixty recipes formatted for printing, with the conversion charts and pantry guide included.
  • Seasonal meal-plan bundles, $9 each in USD. Four weeks of plans, printable recipe cards, editable shopping lists and a prep schedule.
  • Membership, $5 per month in USD. Ad-free browsing, printable recipe cards and saved collections.

These are our own products, so there is no third-party commission involved. Nothing on the free site is withheld to push them. Terms for purchases are in the terms of use.

Newsletter sponsorship

Our weekly newsletter carries at most one sponsor slot per issue, clearly marked as a sponsor slot. The rest of the email is editorial and written by us. We do not send emails on a sponsor's behalf and we never give a sponsor your email address. Rates and the media kit are on the Work With Us page.

What we will never do

  • Publish a paid review presented as an independent one.
  • Accept payment to change a recipe's method, temperature or verdict.
  • Bury a disclosure at the bottom of a page or inside a footer link alone.
  • Sell links inside existing editorial content.
  • Accept sponsorship from supplement, weight-loss or meal-replacement companies.
  • Make a health, medical or therapeutic claim about any product or food.

Questions

If a disclosure on this site is unclear, or you think we have got one wrong, please tell us. We would rather fix it than defend it.
