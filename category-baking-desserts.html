Baking & desserts

Five bakes. Gram weights beside every cup measure, oven temperatures in °F with the °C equivalent, and a convection adjustment written into each one.

Baking is the one part of cooking where measuring loosely genuinely changes the outcome. A cup of all-purpose flour can weigh anywhere from 120 to 150 grams depending on whether you scooped it or spooned it, and a 25 percent swing in flour is the difference between a chewy cookie and a dry one.

So every recipe in this collection gives you the gram weight for flour and sugar alongside the cup measure. You do not need a scale to bake from this site, but a $15 digital scale is the single best thing a home baker can buy, and we say so on the equipment page.

Oven temperatures appear as, for example, 350°F (175°C), with a note on convection where it matters. As a rule, reduce a convection oven by 25°F (about 15°C) and start checking five minutes early.

Overnight Cinnamon Morning Rolls

Prep time
40 minutes
Cook time
28 minutes
Total time
2 hours 30 minutes plus an overnight rise
Yield
12 rolls
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Wheat, milk, eggs.

Shaped the night before and baked straight from the refrigerator in the morning, which is the only way anybody actually eats homemade cinnamon rolls on a weekday. The cold overnight rise also improves the flavor, because the dough has hours to ferment slowly instead of racing at room temperature.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the dough

Milk above 120°F (49°C) kills yeast. If you have no thermometer, it should feel like warm bath water, not hot.

twoFor the filling

threeFor the glaze

Method

  1. Make the dough

    Whisk the warm milk, yeast and sugar and leave 5 minutes until foamy. Add the eggs and melted butter, then the flour and salt. Knead by hand for 8 minutes or in a stand mixer with the dough hook for 5, until smooth and slightly tacky but not sticky.

  2. First rise

    Cover and leave in a warm spot for 60 to 90 minutes, until doubled. In a cold kitchen this can take two hours; go by the dough, not the clock.

  3. Roll and fill

    Roll the dough into a rectangle roughly 16 by 12 inches. Spread the soft butter right to the edges, then scatter the brown sugar, cinnamon and salt evenly over it.

  4. Shape

    Roll up from the long side into a tight log. Cut into 12 pieces with unflavored dental floss slid underneath and pulled through, which does not squash the spiral the way a knife does. Arrange cut side up in a buttered 9 by 13 inch pan.

  5. Overnight rest

    Cover tightly and refrigerate 8 to 16 hours.

  6. Bake

    Take the pan out and let it sit at room temperature for 45 minutes while the oven heats to 350°F (175°C), or 325°F (160°C) on convection. Bake 26 to 30 minutes until the tops are golden and the center roll reads 190°F (88°C).

  7. Glaze

    Beat the cream cheese, powdered sugar, milk and vanilla until smooth. Spread over the rolls while they are still warm but not hot, about 10 minutes out of the oven.

Cook's tips

  • Cut with dental floss, not a knife. A knife compresses the log and the spiral bakes shut. Floss keeps the layers open, which is what gives you the pull-apart middle.
  • Do not skip the 45-minute counter rest. Cold dough straight into the oven bakes unevenly and the centers stay doughy. This is the most common failure with overnight rolls.
  • Take the internal temperature. Enriched doughs are done at 190°F (88°C). Judging by color alone gives you rolls that look right and are raw in the middle.
  • Glaze warm, not hot. Hot rolls melt the glaze straight into the pan; cold rolls will not take it at all.

Storage and reheating. Best on the day, but they keep 2 days at room temperature in an airtight container. Unbaked shaped rolls freeze for 6 weeks; thaw overnight in the refrigerator, then rest 90 minutes at room temperature before baking. Reheat baked rolls at 300°F (150°C) for 8 minutes.

Weekend Country Loaf

Prep time
25 minutes active
Cook time
45 minutes
Total time
About 4 hours
Yield
1 loaf
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Wheat.

No starter, no scale-obsessive percentages, no week of feeding anything. This is a straight yeasted dough with a long, cool rise and a very hot Dutch oven, which is the combination that gives you a dark crust and an open crumb at home. If you have never baked bread, start here.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the dough

Bread flour has more protein than all-purpose and gives noticeably better structure. All-purpose works; the loaf will be a little flatter.

twoFor shaping and baking

Method

  1. Mix

    Stir the flour, yeast and salt together, add the water, and mix with a spatula until no dry flour remains. It will look shaggy and wrong. Cover and leave 30 minutes.

  2. Fold, four times

    With a wet hand, reach under one side of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl a quarter and repeat, four folds total. Do this once every 30 minutes for two hours. The dough will go from ragged to smooth and springy.

  3. Bulk rise

    Cover and leave until the dough has grown by roughly half and is domed and full of bubbles. This takes 1 to 2 hours depending on your kitchen.

  4. Shape

    Tip onto a lightly floured counter, fold the edges into the center to build tension, then flip seam side down and drag the ball toward you a few times to tighten the surface. Rest it seam side up in a bowl lined with a rice-floured towel.

  5. Final proof

    45 minutes at room temperature, or up to 14 hours in the refrigerator for more flavor. A cold-proofed loaf is easier to score.

  6. Bake

    Heat a Dutch oven with its lid at 475°F (245°C) for 45 minutes. Turn the dough onto parchment, score once decisively about half an inch deep, lower it in, cover, and bake 25 minutes. Remove the lid and bake 18 to 22 minutes more until very dark brown and the interior reads 205°F to 210°F (96°C to 99°C).

  7. Cool completely

    Cool on a rack for at least 2 hours before cutting. The crumb is still setting; slicing early gives you a gummy interior.

Cook's tips

  • Heat the Dutch oven empty for a full 45 minutes. The steam trapped by the lid is what gives you oven spring, and a cold pot absorbs the heat that should be going into the loaf.
  • Score once, deep and confident. Timid shallow cuts close up. One decisive slash at a shallow angle gives the loaf somewhere to expand.
  • Bake it darker than feels comfortable. Most home loaves are pulled pale. The color is flavor, and a properly dark crust is the biggest single improvement available to a beginner.
  • Use rice flour on the proofing towel. Wheat flour absorbs moisture and glues the dough to the cloth; rice flour stays powdery.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 days cut side down on a board, no bag. A plastic bag softens the crust within hours. Slice and freeze for up to 3 months and toast straight from frozen. Never refrigerate bread; it goes stale several times faster at refrigerator temperature.

Lemon Meringue Pie

Prep time
45 minutes
Cook time
35 minutes
Total time
3 hours including chilling
Yield
8 slices
Difficulty
Hard
Allergens
Wheat, eggs, milk.

The three failures of a lemon meringue pie are a soggy base, a weeping meringue and a curd that will not set. All three are solvable and none of them is about luck. Blind bake properly, put the meringue onto hot filling, and cook the curd to a temperature rather than to a guess.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the crust

twoFor the lemon curd

threeFor the meringue

Not a speck of yolk in the whites, and a scrupulously clean bowl. Fat prevents whites from whipping.

Method

  1. Make and chill the pastry

    Rub or pulse the butter into the flour, sugar and salt until it looks like coarse breadcrumbs with some pea-sized pieces left. Add the ice water a tablespoon at a time until it just holds together. Flatten into a disc, wrap, and refrigerate 1 hour.

  2. Blind bake

    Roll out and line a 9-inch pie dish. Freeze 20 minutes. Line with parchment, fill with pie weights or dried beans, and bake at 400°F (200°C) for 20 minutes. Remove the weights and bake 10 to 12 minutes more until the base is dry and pale gold. This step is what stops the soggy bottom.

  3. Cook the curd

    Whisk the sugar and cornstarch in a saucepan, then whisk in the water. Bring to a boil over medium heat, whisking constantly, until thick and translucent, about 2 minutes. Temper by whisking a ladle of the hot mixture into the yolks, then return everything to the pan. Cook 2 minutes more, then take off the heat and whisk in the lemon juice, zest, butter and salt.

  4. Fill while hot

    Pour the hot curd into the baked shell. Leave it there while you make the meringue; it must still be steaming when the meringue goes on.

  5. Make the meringue

    Whip the whites with the cream of tartar to soft peaks, then add the sugar one tablespoon at a time, beating until stiff and glossy and the sugar has fully dissolved. Rub a little between your fingers; if it feels gritty, keep going. Fold in the cornstarch at the end.

  6. Seal and bake

    Spread the meringue over the hot filling, pushing it right up against the crust all the way round so there is no gap. Swirl the top. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 15 to 18 minutes until the peaks are deep gold.

  7. Cool slowly

    Cool on a rack for 1 hour at room temperature, then refrigerate 2 hours before slicing. Cooling it fast in the refrigerator makes the meringue shrink and crack.

Cook's tips

  • Spread the meringue onto hot filling and seal it to the crust. A cold filling leaves an uncooked layer underneath that turns to liquid, which is exactly what weeping is.
  • Dissolve the sugar completely. Undissolved sugar draws moisture out of the whites and forms beads on the surface. Add it slowly and test between your fingers.
  • Use fresh lemon juice, never bottled. Bottled juice is pasteurised and carries a flat, slightly metallic note that dominates a curd this simple.
  • Cut with a hot, wet knife. Dip the blade in hot water and wipe it between every slice. Meringue drags on a dry blade.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 2 days refrigerated, loosely covered. Meringue softens over time, so it is best on day one. Do not freeze; the curd separates and the meringue collapses.

Chocolate Buttercream Layer Cake

Prep time
40 minutes
Cook time
32 minutes
Total time
2 hours including cooling
Yield
12 slices
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Wheat, milk, eggs.

A two-layer chocolate cake that uses hot coffee to bloom the cocoa, which deepens the chocolate flavor without making the cake taste of coffee. The buttercream is a straightforward American buttercream stabilised with a little cocoa and salt so it holds its shape in a warm room.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the cake

Natural cocoa is acidic and reacts with the baking soda. Dutch-process cocoa is not, and the cake will not rise the same way.

twoFor the buttercream

Method

  1. Prepare the pans

    Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C), or 325°F (160°C) on convection. Butter two 8-inch round pans, line the bases with parchment, and dust the sides with cocoa rather than flour so there are no white streaks.

  2. Mix the dry ingredients

    Whisk the flour, cocoa, sugar, baking soda, baking powder and salt in a large bowl until uniform and no cocoa lumps remain.

  3. Add the wet

    Whisk in the eggs, buttermilk, oil and vanilla until smooth. Then pour in the hot coffee and whisk. The batter will be alarmingly thin. That is correct; do not add flour.

  4. Bake

    Divide between the pans and bake 30 to 34 minutes, until a skewer in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool completely, at least an hour.

  5. Make the buttercream

    Beat the butter alone for 3 minutes until pale. Add the powdered sugar and cocoa in three additions, beating between each. Add the cream, vanilla and salt and beat 2 minutes more until light. If it looks too stiff, add cream a teaspoon at a time.

  6. Assemble

    Level the tops with a serrated knife. Set the first layer cut side up, spread a generous cup of buttercream, add the second layer cut side down, then coat the outside with a thin crumb coat. Refrigerate 20 minutes, then apply the final coat and swirl.

Cook's tips

  • Weigh the flour, or spoon and level it. Scooping the cup into the bag can add 30 grams, which is the difference between a tender cake and a dry one.
  • Do not open the oven before 28 minutes. A cake this liquid depends on undisturbed steam for its rise.
  • Beat the butter alone first. Three minutes before any sugar goes in gives you a buttercream that is pale and light instead of dense and greasy.
  • Crumb coat and chill. Twenty minutes in the refrigerator between the thin coat and the final one is what stops crumbs dragging through your finish.

Storage and reheating. Frosted cake keeps 3 days at cool room temperature under a dome, or 5 days refrigerated. Bring refrigerated cake back to room temperature for an hour before serving or the buttercream will be hard. Unfrosted layers freeze well wrapped for 2 months.

Oven temperatures and convection

Home ovens commonly run 15°F to 25°F off, and the dial is only ever a statement of intent. An oven thermometer costs about $8 and will tell you more about your baking than any recipe will.

What decides success in this category

Baking fails in a small number of repeatable ways. These four accounted for most of the rounds we had to redo.

  • Flour measured by volume. A scooped cup can weigh anywhere from 120 g to 155 g. That spread is the difference between a tender crumb and a dry one, so use the gram column.
  • Butter at the wrong temperature. Creaming wants butter near 65°F. Colder and it will not hold air, warmer and the structure slumps in the oven.
  • An oven that is not the temperature on the dial. Preheat a full 20 minutes and check with a thermometer before anything goes in.
  • Opening the door early. Cakes and bread set on a schedule. Every look costs heat, and a rising loaf can drop and stay dropped.

Food safety in baking

Raw flour and raw egg are both unsafe to eat. FDA and USDA guidance is that raw flour has been linked to foodborne illness, so do not taste raw cookie dough or cake batter and do not let children eat it. Meringue baked at 350°F for 15 minutes reaches a safe temperature; uncooked meringue does not. Cream cheese frosting and custard fillings must be refrigerated within two hours.
