BEFORE YOU WRITE

The five questions we get most

These come in often enough that the answer is already settled. Reading them first will usually save you a wait.

  • A recipe did not work. Give us the oven temperature you set, the pan you used and its diameter, the rack position, and what the food looked like at the moment it went off. That is normally enough for us to reproduce the fault on the bench. If two or more people report the same thing, it goes back into testing.
  • Can I republish your recipe on my site? No. You can link to it freely and quote a sentence or two with attribution. See the terms of use.
  • Can I print a recipe for a class or a community supper? Yes, for non-commercial use, with the source noted on the handout.
  • Will you review my product? Read Work With Us first. We say yes rarely, we test for at least one full cycle before we write anything, and we state in the piece when a product was sent to us.
  • Nutrition questions. We cannot advise on individual dietary needs. See the nutrition and allergen disclaimer and speak to a qualified health professional.

Most other questions are answered on the FAQ page.

What a useful failure report contains

We can only retest what we can reproduce, so the reports that get fixed fastest read like lab notes. Give us six things: the recipe name, the oven temperature you set and whether you have checked it against a separate thermometer, the pan material and diameter in inches, the rack position counted from the bottom, the elapsed time at the point it went wrong, and one sentence describing what you saw rather than what you expected. Photographs of the failure are more useful to us than photographs of a success. If you measured an internal temperature, send the reading.
