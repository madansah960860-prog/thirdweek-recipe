Vegetarian & vegan

Six plant-forward recipes built on beans, grains and whatever is good in the Pacific Northwest this month. The vegan swap is written into every one, not left for you to work out.

These recipes are here because they taste good, not because of what they leave out. We do not describe food as clean, guilt-free or virtuous on this site, and we do not think a meal without meat needs a justification. What a vegetarian dinner does need is a plan for texture and savouriness, because those two things are what people actually miss.

The reliable answers are browning, acid and fat. Chickpeas crisped in a dry pan, kale massaged until it softens, tahini thinned with lemon, tomatoes salted so they release their juice. Each of the recipes below leans on at least one of those.

Where a recipe contains dairy or eggs, the vegan substitution is written into the recipe rather than left to you. Where a recipe is naturally vegan, it says so in the allergen line.

Chickpea and Avocado Grain Bowl

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
30 minutes
Yield
2 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Sesame. Vegan as written.

This is a template more than a recipe. A grain, a warm roasted thing, a crunchy raw thing, a protein and a dressing with enough acid to hold it together. Once you have the tahini dressing in the refrigerator, a bowl takes ten minutes and uses whatever is in the crisper drawer.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the bowl

twoFor the tahini dressing

Tahini seizes into a solid paste when you first add liquid. Keep whisking and adding water; it comes back smooth.

Method

  1. Cook the grain

    Cook the quinoa or farro according to the package in salted water. Drain and spread on a plate to cool slightly so it does not steam the vegetables.

  2. Crisp the chickpeas

    Dry the chickpeas thoroughly, then toss with the olive oil, smoked paprika and salt. Cook in a dry skillet over medium-high for 8 minutes, shaking often, until they blister and pop.

  3. Make the dressing

    Whisk the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt. It will seize. Add the water one tablespoon at a time, whisking, until it is pourable and pale.

  4. Assemble

    Divide the grain between two bowls. Arrange the chickpeas, avocado, cabbage, radish and arugula in sections rather than mixing them, so each bite can be different. Spoon the dressing over.

Cook's tips

  • Dry the chickpeas properly before they hit the pan. Wet chickpeas steam and go soft. Five minutes on a towel makes the difference between crisp and mushy.
  • Make a double batch of dressing. Tahini dressing keeps a week and improves roasted vegetables, eggs, sandwiches and salads. It is the most useful thing in our refrigerator.
  • Arrange, do not toss. Sections keep the textures distinct. Tossed bowls turn uniform within a minute and lose the point.
  • Cook the grain in advance. Two cups of cooked farro on Sunday is four bowls during the week.

Storage and reheating. Components keep 4 days refrigerated in separate containers. Dressing keeps 7 days; thin it with water when it thickens. Do not store sliced avocado; cut it fresh. Not suitable for freezing.

Panzanella with Torn Mozzarella

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
10 minutes
Total time
40 minutes including resting
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Wheat, milk.

Panzanella exists because Tuscan cooks refused to throw away stale bread, and it only works with bread that is genuinely dry. Fresh bread turns to mush in ten minutes. The other rule is the resting time: thirty minutes on the counter is when the bread drinks the tomato juice and the salad becomes itself.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the salad

Use the best tomatoes you can find and do not refrigerate them. Cold destroys tomato aroma and it does not come back.

twoFor the dressing

Method

  1. Salt the tomatoes

    Put the tomato chunks in a colander set over a bowl, toss with the salt, and leave 20 minutes. Keep the juice that collects; it is the most flavourful liquid in the dish.

  2. Toast the bread

    If your bread is not properly stale, spread the torn pieces on a sheet pan and dry them in a 300°F (150°C) oven for 10 minutes. They should be dry through, not browned.

  3. Make the dressing

    Whisk the collected tomato juice with the olive oil, vinegar, garlic and pepper.

  4. Combine and wait

    Toss the bread, tomatoes, onion and cucumber with the dressing. Leave on the counter for 30 minutes, tossing once halfway. This is not optional; it is where the dish happens.

  5. Finish

    Just before serving, fold through the torn mozzarella and basil. Taste and add salt if it needs it, which it usually does.

Cook's tips

  • Tear the bread, do not cube it. Torn edges have more surface area and soak up dressing unevenly, which is what you want. Neat cubes stay uniformly soggy.
  • Keep the tomato juice. Draining salted tomatoes and throwing the liquid away is discarding the best part of the dressing.
  • Add the mozzarella and basil last. Both go gray and rubbery if they sit in vinegar for half an hour.
  • Never refrigerate the tomatoes. Below about 55°F (13°C) tomatoes stop producing the compounds that make them smell like tomatoes.

Storage and reheating. Best within 2 hours of assembly. It keeps 1 day refrigerated but the bread goes soft and the texture is lost. Bring back to room temperature before eating. Does not freeze.

Farfalle with Basil and Walnut Pesto

Prep time
10 minutes
Cook time
10 minutes
Total time
20 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Wheat, milk, tree nuts.

Pine nuts cost roughly four times what walnuts cost and, in a sauce with this much basil and garlic, most people cannot tell. Walnuts bring a slight bitterness that we actually prefer. The important technique here is that pesto is never cooked; it is loosened with pasta water off the heat.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the pesto

twoFor the pasta

Method

  1. Toast the walnuts

    Toast the walnuts in a dry skillet over medium for 4 minutes, shaking, until fragrant. Cool completely; warm nuts will wilt the basil in the processor.

  2. Blitz the pesto

    Pulse the basil, walnuts, garlic and salt in a food processor until coarse. Add the Parmesan and pulse twice. With the motor running, pour the olive oil in a thin stream. Stir in the lemon juice by hand at the end.

  3. Cook the pasta

    Cook the farfalle in well-salted water to al dente. Reserve a cup of the water and drain.

  4. Combine off the heat

    Put the pasta in a warm bowl, not a hot pan. Add the pesto and a splash of pasta water and toss until every piece is coated. Fold in the tomatoes.

Cook's tips

  • Never cook pesto. Heat turns basil gray and bitter within a minute. The pasta is the only heat it should meet.
  • Pulse, do not blend. A continuously running blade bruises the basil and heats the oil. Short pulses keep the sauce green.
  • Reserve more pasta water than you think. Pesto tightens as it sits and you will want to loosen it again at the table.
  • Freeze pesto in an ice cube tray. One cube is roughly enough for a single serving. Cover the surface with a thin film of oil before freezing to keep it green.

Storage and reheating. Pesto keeps 5 days refrigerated with a layer of olive oil on top, or 3 months frozen. Dressed pasta keeps 2 days refrigerated and is good cold. Do not reheat in a pan; loosen with a little hot water instead.

Roasted Vegetable Traybake with Chickpeas

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
30 minutes
Total time
45 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Vegan and gluten-free as written.

The point of a traybake is that the ingredient list is a suggestion. What matters is grouping vegetables by how long they take, so nothing is raw and nothing is charcoal. Dense roots go in first, soft vegetables go in later, and leaves go in for the last three minutes.

Ingredient checklist

oneThe slow group, in first

twoThe fast group, in after 15 minutes

threeSpice mix and finish

Method

  1. Heat the oven high

    425°F (220°C), or 400°F (200°C) on convection, with a rack in the upper third.

  2. Roast the slow group

    Toss the sweet potato and red onion with the olive oil, salt and half the spice mix. Spread in a single layer on a large sheet pan with gaps between pieces. Roast 15 minutes.

  3. Add the fast group

    Toss the broccoli, pepper and dried chickpeas with the remaining oil and spices. Add to the pan, spreading everything out again. Roast 15 to 18 minutes more.

  4. Finish

    Everything should be tender with charred edges. Squeeze over the lemon juice, scatter the cilantro, and drizzle with the extra-virgin olive oil.

Cook's tips

  • Use two pans if one looks full. Crowding is the only real way to ruin a traybake. Vegetables need air around them or they steam in their own moisture.
  • Group by density, not by recipe order. Roots take 30 minutes, brassicas take 18, peppers take 15, spinach takes 3. Put them in accordingly.
  • Dry the chickpeas. Wet chickpeas will not crisp, and crisp chickpeas are the best thing on the tray.
  • Finish with acid and raw oil. Roasted vegetables are sweet and need something sharp. Lemon at the end, always.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 4 days refrigerated. Excellent cold in a grain bowl or folded into eggs. Reheat at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes rather than microwaving, which turns everything soft. Freezing is not recommended; the texture collapses.

Kale and Walnut Chopped Salad

Prep time
20 minutes
Cook time
None
Total time
20 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Milk, tree nuts. Vegan without the cheese.

The one salad that survives being packed for lunch. Kale leaves do not wilt in dressing the way lettuce does, and massaging them for two minutes breaks down the cell walls enough that the texture goes from tough to pleasant. This salad is genuinely better an hour after you make it.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the salad

twoFor the mustard vinaigrette

Method

  1. Strip and shred the kale

    Hold the stem at the base and pull the leaf away with your other hand. Stack the leaves, roll them into a cigar, and slice as thinly as you can. Thin ribbons are much easier to eat than torn pieces.

  2. Massage it

    Put the shredded kale in a large bowl with the salt and squeeze it firmly between your hands for 2 minutes. It will darken, shrink by about a third and soften noticeably.

  3. Make the vinaigrette

    Whisk the vinegar, mustard, maple syrup, salt and pepper, then stream in the olive oil while whisking until it thickens.

  4. Combine

    Add the carrot, onion and half the dressing to the kale and toss. Leave 10 minutes, then add the walnuts, feta, cranberries and remaining dressing.

Cook's tips

  • Massage for the full two minutes. Thirty seconds does nothing. You are physically breaking down the leaf, and you can feel when it happens.
  • Slice the kale thinner than feels necessary. Quarter-inch ribbons. Large pieces of kale are hard work to eat and the dressing never gets into them.
  • Dress it early. This is the opposite of every other salad rule. Kale improves with an hour in dressing, which is exactly why it travels.
  • Toast the walnuts and cool them. Warm nuts turn the feta greasy and wilt the onion.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 days refrigerated and is best on day two. Add the walnuts fresh each day if you want them crunchy. Not suitable for freezing.

Building a vegetarian dinner that fills you up

The complaint about vegetarian dinners is almost always that they stop being satisfying an hour later. In testing that turned out to be a structural problem rather than a nutritional one, and it has three fixes.

  • Include a legume or a grain with body. Chickpeas, white beans, lentils, farro and barley all have enough substance to anchor a plate. A salad of leaves and vegetables alone will not.
  • Do not skimp on fat. Olive oil, tahini, nuts, avocado and cheese carry flavor and slow a meal down. A dressing made with three tablespoons of oil is not a failure of restraint, it is the recipe.
  • Brown something. Roasted, charred or crisped elements supply the savory depth that meat usually provides. A tray of vegetables at 425°F does more for a meal than any amount of careful seasoning at 350°F.

We are a cooking site and not a nutrition service, so we do not make dietary or health claims. If you are planning a fully plant-based diet, particularly for children or during pregnancy, talk to a registered dietitian. Our nutrition and allergen disclaimer explains our position.

What decides success in this category

The versions that failed in testing failed for structural reasons, not for want of seasoning. These four came up again and again.

  • No anchor on the plate. Leaves and raw vegetables alone read as a side dish. A legume or a grain with body is what turns it into dinner.
  • Vegetables crowded on the tray. A packed pan steams. Spread everything to a single layer at 425°F or you lose the browning that stands in for meat.
  • Fat cut back. Dressings trimmed from three tablespoons of oil down to one tested flat every time. The fat is what carries the flavor.
  • A vegan swap invented at the stove. Coconut yogurt, silken tofu and cashew cream behave differently under heat. Use the swap written into the recipe, because that is the one we cooked.
