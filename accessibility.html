Accessibility statement

What we have built to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA, the limitations we know about, how we test, and how to tell us when something does not work.

Last updated 09/14/2026. This page is written in plain English wherever we can manage it. If anything here is unclear, ask us and we will explain it and probably rewrite it.

Our commitment

We want this site to work for everyone who wants to cook from it, including people using screen readers, keyboard navigation, screen magnification, voice control, or a browser configured to reduce motion or increase text size.

We aim to meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA. We believe the site substantially conforms to that standard, and we treat any gap as a bug rather than an acceptable state.

What this means in practice

You can reach every recipe on this site with a keyboard alone, at 400 percent zoom, with animation switched off, and with a screen reader reading the method in order. Body text holds a contrast ratio of 4.5:1 or better against its background. No recipe, method or article is hidden behind JavaScript. Where we fall short, the known limitations below name the gap instead of burying it. If you hit something that does not work, the contact routes further down this page reach a person in the Portland test kitchen, and the report is logged as a bug with a deadline on it.

What we have done

  • Semantic structure. Real landmarks throughout: header, navigation, main, aside and footer. Headings run in order without skipping levels, so a screen reader can outline any page.
  • Skip to content. A skip link is the first thing in the tab order on every page and becomes visible when focused.
  • Full keyboard operation. Every control, including the menus, the accordions, the cookie banner, the unit toggle and the servings scaler, works from the keyboard alone. Dropdowns open on Enter or Space, respond to arrow keys and close on Escape, returning focus to the trigger.
  • Visible focus. A high-contrast focus ring appears on every interactive element. We have not removed default focus styles anywhere.
  • Contrast. Body text meets or exceeds 4.5:1 against its background and large text meets 3:1. Our decorative brand colors are never used for body copy for exactly this reason.
  • Text alternatives. Every photograph has descriptive alt text that says what is actually in the picture. Decorative elements are hidden from assistive technology.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label, not a placeholder pretending to be one. Errors appear next to the field they concern, in text as well as color, and the first field with a problem receives focus on submission.
  • Reduced motion. If your system asks for reduced motion, every animation, transition and moving pattern on the site is disabled. Nothing on this site depends on motion to be understood.
  • Color is never the only signal. Errors, states and categories carry text or shape as well as color.
  • Touch targets. Interactive elements are at least 44 by 44 pixels with spacing between them.
  • Zoom and reflow. The site reflows to a single column and remains usable at 320 pixels wide and at 400 percent zoom, with no horizontal scrolling and no loss of content.
  • No traps. No content that blocks the page on load, no auto-playing audio or video, no automatic redirects, and nothing that interferes with your browser's back button.
  • Content that does not need JavaScript to be read. Recipes, methods and articles are in the page markup. Scripts add the unit toggle, the scaler and the entrance animations, and their absence does not hide content.
  • Printing. Every recipe has a print stylesheet that removes navigation, advertising and decoration, leaving the ingredients, method and notes in a readable size.

Known limitations

We would rather list these than pretend they do not exist.

  • Third-party advertising. Advertisements are supplied by external vendors and we do not control their markup. Some ad creatives may have poor color contrast or missing text alternatives. We can raise problems with our ad partner and we do, but we cannot fix an individual advertisement ourselves. Reader-supported membership removes advertising entirely.
  • Photographs of food. Alt text describes what is in the picture. It cannot convey everything a photograph communicates about texture or color, so we try to put anything essential into the recipe text as well.
  • Complex tables. Our conversion and temperature tables are wide. On a narrow screen they scroll horizontally inside their own container. The page itself never scrolls sideways, but the table does, and we know that is not ideal for everyone.
  • Older archived content. Anything published before mid-2025 may not fully meet the standard. We are working through it and correcting as we go.

How we test

  • Keyboard-only navigation of every page template before it ships.
  • Screen reader spot checks using the built-in readers on Windows and macOS.
  • Automated contrast and markup checks on every page.
  • Testing at 320 pixels wide and at 400 percent zoom.
  • Testing with reduced motion enabled at the operating system level.

Automated tools catch roughly a third of accessibility problems, so the manual checks are the ones that matter, and the reports we get from readers matter more still.

Tell us about a problem

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible for you to use, please tell us. It is genuinely useful and we treat it as a priority.

  • Use the contact form and choose the accessibility subject.
  • Or email hello@saltgrainkitchen.com with Accessibility in the subject line.
  • Or call +1 (503) 555-0184, Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm Pacific Time.

It helps if you can tell us the page address, what you were trying to do, what happened instead, and what browser and assistive technology you were using. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within two business days and to tell you what we are going to do about it within ten.

If you need a recipe in a different format, ask and we will send it to you.

Formal approvals

This statement reflects Saltgrain Kitchen Co.'s own assessment of saltgrainkitchen.com, prepared and last reviewed on 09/14/2026. The site has not been through a formal third-party accessibility audit, and we would rather say so than imply a certification we do not hold. We review this statement at least every six months, and again after any change to a page template. If the position changes we will update this page and the date at the top of it.
