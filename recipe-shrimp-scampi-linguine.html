Skillet Shrimp Scampi Linguine

Twenty-five minutes from cold pan to the table. The sauce clings to the pasta rather than pooling under it because reserved pasta water and cold butter go in at the end, in that order.

Developed and retested in the Saltgrain test kitchen by Priya Raghunathan Published 07/02/2026 Last tested 09/01/2026
Prep time
10 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
25 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Course
Dinner

Quick weeknight Italian at home Allergens: shellfish, wheat, milk

Scampi is four things most kitchens already hold: butter, olive oil, garlic and acid. The variable is not the list, it is the order and the clock. Shrimp are done in about three minutes and turn to erasers by four, so they leave the pan before the sauce is built and go back in once the burner is off.

The second variable is pasta water. That cloudy, starchy liquid is what pulls butter and oil into an emulsion that coats each strand instead of sliding off it. Scoop out half a cup before you drain, every time. If you take one habit from this site, take that one.

The finish happens off the heat, with cold butter and lemon juice. Lemon juice held at a boil goes dull and faintly bitter. Stirred into a pan that has stopped bubbling, it stays sharp. The change costs ten seconds and it was the clearest difference between our second round and our third.

How we tested this

Three rounds, all in the same 12-inch stainless skillet with sloping sides. Round one used minced garlic, which scorched in under thirty seconds while the wine was still reducing, so round two switched to thin slices. Round two also cooked the linguine to full al dente in water, and the sauce sat under the pasta rather than on it. Round three pulled the pasta a minute early, finished it in the skillet with a quarter cup of the cooking water, and added the lemon off the burner. That is the version below. We ran round three once more in a 10-inch pan to see what a small skillet does: there was no room to toss, the sauce never came together, and pan size is now the first thing we ask about when someone tells us their scampi broke.

Method

  1. Get the water going first

    Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and add the salt. Everything else in this recipe takes less time than the pasta, so the pot goes on before you touch anything else.

  2. Dry the shrimp

    Spread the shrimp on paper towels and pat them properly dry on both sides. Wet shrimp steam gray; dry shrimp take on color in ninety seconds. Season them with the salt and pepper.

  3. Cook the pasta one minute short

    Add the linguine and cook one minute less than the package says for al dente. Scoop out ½ cup of pasta water and set it beside the stove, then drain. Do not rinse.

  4. Sear the shrimp and take them out

    While the pasta cooks, heat the olive oil and 1 tablespoon of the butter in a wide skillet over medium-high. Add the shrimp in a single layer and cook 90 seconds per side, until just pink and opaque. Move them to a plate. They will finish cooking later.

  5. Build the sauce

    Lower the heat to medium. Add another tablespoon of butter and the sliced garlic with the red pepper flakes. Cook 60 to 90 seconds, stirring, until the garlic is pale gold at the edges. Do not let it brown or the whole pan turns bitter.

  6. Deglaze

    Pour in the wine and scrape the bottom of the pan. Let it bubble and reduce by about half, which takes two to three minutes and smells like the point of the recipe.

  7. Bring it together

    Add the drained linguine and ¼ cup of the pasta water. Toss hard with tongs for a full minute. The sauce will look thin, then suddenly thicken and coat the strands. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it tightens too far.

  8. Finish off the heat

    Take the pan off the burner. Return the shrimp and any juices from the plate, then add the remaining 2 tablespoons of cold butter, the lemon juice, the zest and the parsley. Toss until the butter disappears into the sauce. Serve immediately, in warmed bowls if you have thought that far ahead.

Food safety

Cook shrimp until the flesh is opaque and firm throughout, an internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) under USDA guidance. Thaw frozen shrimp overnight in the refrigerator or under cold running water, never on the counter, and do not refreeze shrimp that has already been thawed. Shellfish is one of the nine major US food allergens.

Cook's tips

  • Buy shell-on shrimp and peel them yourself. They are usually cheaper per pound and the texture is noticeably firmer. Keep the shells in the freezer; twenty minutes of simmering turns them into a stock that improves the next batch.
  • Slice the garlic, do not mince it. Minced garlic in a hot pan goes from raw to burnt in about twenty seconds. Slices give you a much wider margin and a mellower flavor.
  • Undercook the pasta on purpose. The final minute of cooking happens in the skillet, in the sauce, which is where the pasta picks up flavor. Pasta cooked fully in water and then sauced never tastes as integrated.
  • Warm the bowls. Run them under hot tap water and dry them while the pasta cooks. Butter sauces seize on cold ceramic and the difference is obvious.

What goes wrong

  • Grey shrimp instead of seared ones. They went into the pan wet, or the pan was not hot enough yet. Dry both sides on paper towels and wait for the oil to shimmer before anything goes in.
  • A bitter pan. The garlic browned. Slices, medium heat, 60 to 90 seconds, pale gold at the edges, and no further. Once it is dark the whole sauce carries it.
  • Sauce pooling at the bottom of the bowl. Either the pasta water went down the drain or the skillet is too small to toss in. Reserve half a cup, and use a pan at least 12 inches across.

Substitutions

  • No wine: use ½ cup low-sodium chicken broth plus 1 teaspoon white wine vinegar. Add the vinegar at the end, off the heat, with the lemon.
  • Different pasta: spaghetti, bucatini or angel hair all work. Angel hair cooks in about four minutes, so start the shrimp before the pasta goes in.
  • Dairy-free: replace the butter with a further 3 tablespoons of olive oil. The sauce will be looser and sharper, so use the full ½ cup of pasta water.
  • No shrimp: bay scallops (90 seconds total) or sliced chicken breast (four minutes, cooked to 165°F / 74°C) both slot straight in.
  • Gluten-free: use a bronze-cut gluten-free linguine and reserve extra cooking water; those pastas release less starch, so you need more of it.

Make ahead, storage and reheating

  • Make ahead: shrimp can be peeled, dried and seasoned up to four hours ahead and kept covered in the refrigerator. Garlic can be sliced the same morning. The dish itself does not hold.
  • Storage: refrigerate leftovers in a shallow airtight container within two hours. Cooked shellfish keeps three to four days at 40°F (4°C) or below under USDA guidance.
  • Freezing: not recommended. The emulsion splits on thawing and the shrimp come back rubbery. We tried it twice and stopped.
  • Reheating: warm gently in a skillet over low heat with a tablespoon of water, tossing, for two to three minutes. Microwaving overcooks the shrimp before the pasta is warm.

What you need to cook it

A 12-inch skillet with sloping sides, tongs, and a large pot. A pan any smaller and the pasta will not have room to be tossed, which is the whole technique. See our equipment and pantry guide for what we actually use.
