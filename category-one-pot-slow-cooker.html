One-pot & slow cooker

Six recipes that leave one vessel in the sink. Braises, stews, traybakes, and a slow cooker sauce that stretches across three dinners.

One-pot cooking is not about laziness, it is about layering. Everything that goes into the pot leaves something behind for the next ingredient, so the order matters more than it does when you cook things separately. Brown the meat, soften the aromatics in that fat, cook out the tomato paste in the same pan, deglaze, and by the time liquid goes in you have built four layers of flavor without washing anything.

The other reason to cook this way is leverage. A slow cooker full of ragù on Sunday is Tuesday's baked ziti and Thursday's stuffed peppers. Our meal plans are built around exactly that idea.

Roasted Squash and Feta Soup

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
45 minutes
Total time
1 hour
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Milk. Naturally gluten-free.

Roasting the squash before it goes anywhere near liquid is the entire difference between this soup and the flat, vaguely sweet version most people have had. Twenty extra minutes of dry heat caramelises the sugars and gives you something with actual depth. Boiled squash tastes like boiled squash.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor roasting

twoFor the pot

threeTo finish

Method

  1. Roast everything

    Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the squash, onion and unpeeled garlic with the oil, salt, pepper and coriander on a sheet pan in a single layer. Roast 35 to 40 minutes, turning once, until the edges are dark brown.

  2. Squeeze the garlic

    Squeeze the roasted garlic cloves out of their skins into a large pot and discard the skins. Add the roasted squash and onion.

  3. Simmer briefly

    Add the warm broth and bring to a simmer for 5 minutes. This is enough; the vegetables are already cooked and long simmering only dulls the roasted flavor.

  4. Blend

    Blend until completely smooth, either with an immersion blender in the pot or in batches in a countertop blender with the lid vented and covered with a towel. Hot liquid in a sealed blender expands and will blow the lid off.

  5. Season and serve

    Stir in the vinegar, then taste and adjust the salt. Ladle into bowls and top with feta, toasted pumpkin seeds, olive oil and pepper flakes.

Cook's tips

  • Roast, do not boil. This is the only instruction in the recipe that changes the result dramatically.
  • Add the vinegar at the end. A tablespoon of acid in a sweet vegetable soup is what stops it tasting like baby food. It should not taste sour; it should taste finished.
  • Warm the broth before it goes in. Cold liquid onto hot roasted vegetables drops the pot temperature and you lose the fond stuck to the squash.
  • Vent the blender. Hot soup in a sealed jug is genuinely dangerous. Fill it no more than half full and hold a folded towel over the lid.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 4 days refrigerated, per USDA guidance for cooked vegetable soups. Freezes for 3 months without the feta and seeds. Reheat to a bare simmer over medium-low, at least 165°F (74°C), adding a splash of broth if it has thickened.

Seafood Stew with Fennel and Chili

Prep time
20 minutes
Cook time
25 minutes
Total time
45 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Medium
Allergens
Fish, shellfish. Naturally gluten-free without the bread.

The broth is the recipe and the seafood is almost an afterthought, which sounds wrong until you make it. Fifteen minutes of fennel, garlic, chili and tomato gives you something worth eating on its own. The fish goes in for the last six minutes and no longer.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the broth

twoFor the seafood

Buy shellfish the day you cook it. Discard any that stay open when tapped before cooking, or stay shut after.

threeTo serve

Method

  1. Build the base

    Heat the olive oil in a wide Dutch oven over medium. Cook the fennel and onion for 10 minutes until soft and just golden. Add the garlic and pepper flakes for one minute.

  2. Cook out the tomato paste

    Stir in the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes until it darkens.

  3. Deglaze and simmer

    Add the wine and reduce by half. Add the crushed tomatoes, broth, salt and bay leaf. Simmer uncovered for 15 minutes. Taste it now; this is your last chance to season before the fish goes in.

  4. Add the seafood in order

    Add the mussels or clams, cover, and cook 3 minutes until they open. Discard any that stay shut. Add the fish pieces and shrimp, cover again, and cook 4 to 5 minutes more until the fish flakes and the shrimp are opaque.

  5. Finish

    Turn off the heat, stir in the lemon juice, and scatter the basil and fennel fronds. Serve with the grilled bread for the broth.

Cook's tips

  • Season the broth before the seafood goes in. Once the fish is in the pot, stirring to distribute salt breaks it apart.
  • Add the shellfish first, the delicate fish last. Mussels take three minutes, cod takes five, shrimp take three. Staggering by two minutes is what keeps the fish in whole pieces.
  • Discard shellfish that will not close before cooking or will not open after. This is not superstition; it is the standard FDA and USDA guidance for live shellfish.
  • Do not stir once the fish is in. Shake the pot by the handles if you need to move things. A spoon turns cod into flakes.

Storage and reheating. Best on the day. Keeps 2 days refrigerated, per USDA guidance for cooked shellfish and fish, in a covered container. Reheat gently to 165°F (74°C) over low heat; the seafood will firm up slightly. Do not freeze.

One-Pot Chicken and Rice with Herbs

Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
40 minutes
Total time
55 minutes
Yield
6 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free as written.

Everything cooks in one pot and the best part is the crust that forms on the bottom. Rinse the rice, brown the chicken properly, and then leave the lid alone for the full twenty minutes. Lifting the lid to check is the reason home-cooked rice comes out unevenly.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the chicken

twoFor the rice

Rinsing removes surface starch. Unrinsed rice in a one-pot dish turns gluey around the chicken.

threeTo finish

Method

  1. Season and brown

    Toss the chicken with the salt, pepper, cumin, coriander and turmeric. Heat the oil in a wide, heavy pot over medium-high and brown the chicken skin side down for 6 minutes, then 3 minutes on the other side. Move to a plate.

  2. Cook the onions properly

    Lower the heat to medium and cook the sliced onions in the chicken fat for 12 minutes, stirring, until deeply golden. This is the flavor base and it cannot be rushed. Add the garlic for one minute.

  3. Toast the rice

    Add the drained rice and stir for 2 minutes until the grains look translucent at the edges.

  4. Add liquid and chicken

    Pour in the warm broth, add the cinnamon stick and salt, and stir once. Nestle the chicken back in skin side up along with any juices. Bring to a simmer.

  5. Cover and leave it alone

    Cover tightly, reduce to the lowest heat, and cook 20 minutes without lifting the lid. Then turn off the heat and let it stand, still covered, for 10 minutes.

  6. Serve

    Check the chicken has reached 165°F (74°C). Fluff the rice with a fork, scatter the mint and cilantro, and serve with lemon wedges.

Cook's tips

  • Rinse the rice until the water is clear. Three or four changes of water. This takes ninety seconds and decides the texture of the whole dish.
  • Give the onions twelve real minutes. Most of the flavor in this pot comes from properly browned onion, and four minutes gets you nowhere near it.
  • Do not lift the lid. Every peek releases the steam that is cooking the top layer of rice, and you end up with hard grains on top and mush at the bottom.
  • Warm the broth first. Cold broth stops the pot dead and adds ten minutes to the cook, by which point the bottom layer has overcooked.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 to 4 days refrigerated, in line with USDA guidance for cooked poultry and rice. Cool rice quickly and refrigerate within one hour; cooked rice left at room temperature can grow bacteria that survive reheating. Reheat with a splash of broth, covered, to 165°F (74°C). Freezes for 2 months.

One-Pot Herbed Couscous with Green Beans

Prep time
10 minutes
Cook time
15 minutes
Total time
25 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Allergens
Wheat. Vegan as written.

Couscous is not a grain, it is very small pasta, which is why it needs steaming rather than boiling and why it takes five minutes. This version cooks the green beans in the same pot, off the same heat, so there is genuinely one pan involved.

Ingredient checklist

oneFor the pot

twoFor the couscous

The ratio is a little more than one part liquid to one part couscous by volume. Too much liquid and it goes to paste.

threeTo finish

Method

  1. Cook the vegetables

    Heat the olive oil in a wide pot with a lid over medium. Cook the onion 5 minutes, add the garlic and cumin for one minute, then add the green beans, tomatoes and salt. Cook 6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the beans are bright and just tender.

  2. Add the couscous

    Stir the dry couscous through the vegetables so every grain is coated in oil.

  3. Add boiling liquid and cover

    Pour over the boiling broth, add the salt, stir once, cover tightly and take the pot off the heat entirely. Leave it for 5 minutes.

  4. Fluff and finish

    Fork through the couscous to separate the grains. Stir in the parsley, mint, lemon juice and extra-virgin olive oil. Top with toasted almonds and serve warm or at room temperature.

Cook's tips

  • Take the pot off the heat before the couscous steams. Residual heat is enough. Leaving it on the burner scorches the bottom layer.
  • Coat the couscous in oil before the liquid goes in. This is what keeps the grains separate rather than clumped.
  • Toast the almonds in a dry pan for four minutes. Raw almonds add crunch; toasted almonds add flavor, and the difference is out of all proportion to the effort.
  • Add the herbs after fluffing. Herbs stirred into steaming couscous go black and lose their perfume.

Storage and reheating. Keeps 3 days refrigerated in an airtight container and is genuinely good cold, packed for lunch. Add a squeeze of lemon before eating to wake it up. Does not freeze well; the couscous turns mealy.

Slow cooker safety, briefly

Slow cookers are safe used the way they were designed to be used. The rules are short and they are not negotiable.

  • Never start with frozen meat. The pot spends too long in the 40°F to 140°F (4°C to 60°C) range where bacteria multiply fastest. Thaw in the refrigerator first.
  • Fill between half and three quarters full. Under half and the food overcooks; over three quarters and it may not reach a safe temperature.
  • Do not lift the lid. Each peek costs roughly 20 minutes of cooking time as the pot climbs back to temperature.
  • Do not use the slow cooker to reheat leftovers. It heats too slowly. Reheat on the stove or in the microwave to 165°F (74°C), then hold in the slow cooker if you need to.
  • Cool leftovers in shallow containers and refrigerate within two hours. A full ceramic insert in the refrigerator stays warm for hours.

This follows USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service guidance on slow cookers and food safety.

What decides success in this category

One vessel means every mistake stays in the pot. These four cost us the most retests.

  • Skipping the browning. The fond on the bottom of the pot is most of the finished flavor. Ten minutes of searing is not an optional step.
  • Too much liquid. A covered pot loses very little to evaporation. Start lower than a stovetop recipe would and reduce at the end if you need to.
  • Everything in at once. Dense roots and quick vegetables do not share a timeline. Stagger them, or accept that one of the two will be wrong.
  • Salting to taste at the start. A long reduction concentrates whatever is already in the pot. Season lightly early, then correct off the heat.
