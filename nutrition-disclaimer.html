Nutrition & allergen disclaimer

Why our nutrition numbers are estimates, what our allergen tags can and cannot tell you, and the food safety guidance we cook to.

Last updated 09/14/2026. This page is written in plain English wherever we can manage it. If anything here is unclear, ask us and we will explain it and probably rewrite it.

Nutrition figures here are estimates

Where a recipe on this site includes nutrition information, it is an approximation, calculated from generic ingredient databases. It is not a laboratory analysis of the food we cooked, and it is not a nutrition facts panel of the kind you see on packaging.

The figures can be wrong in either direction, sometimes by a wide margin, because:

  • Brands differ. Two cans of the same product can carry meaningfully different sodium and sugar.
  • Cuts of meat differ in fat content, even within the same label.
  • Produce varies with variety, ripeness and size.
  • Not all the oil in a pan ends up on the plate, and databases cannot know how much did.
  • Portion size is a judgment. Our four servings may not be your four servings.
  • Substitutions change everything, and most of our recipes offer several.

Always check the packaging in front of you. The label on the product in your hand is accurate for that product. Our estimate is not.

What this means in practice

Read our nutrition numbers the way you would read a weather forecast: useful for direction, wrong in the detail. For anything that matters medically, use the label on the package in your hand. Treat an allergen tag as a starting point for shopping, never as a clearance to serve. Cook to the internal temperatures listed below rather than to the minutes printed in a recipe, because a thermometer knows your oven and we do not. Where any of this conflicts with advice from your physician or your registered dietitian, follow them and not us.

Why nutrition sits at the bottom of the page

Because it is the least reliable information on the page and we do not want it read as a headline. You will not find calorie counts in our navigation, in our recipe titles or in our card summaries. We do not organize recipes by calorie count and we do not offer a low-calorie category.

This is deliberate. We are a cooking site.

We do not give health or dietary advice

Nothing on this site is medical, nutritional or dietary advice, and none of us is qualified to give it. We do not treat, diagnose, prevent or manage any condition, and we make no claims about what any food does to your body.

If you are managing diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease, celiac disease, a food allergy, pregnancy, an eating disorder, or any other condition where what you eat matters medically, please talk to a physician, a registered dietitian or another qualified health professional. They can look at your actual circumstances, which a website cannot.

If you are helping someone in distress around food, in the United States the National Eating Disorders Association operates a helpline and referral service.

How we write about food

You will not find the words clean, guilt-free, sinful, detox, superfood or fat-burning anywhere on this site, and if one slips through, it is a mistake and we would like to know about it.

Food is not moral. A cookie is not a sin and a salad is not a virtue. We think a recipe site that assigns moral weight to ingredients does its readers real harm, and we would rather just tell you how to make the thing taste good.

Allergen tags

Recipes carry allergen tags describing the recipe as written, against the nine major food allergens recognized in the United States under the FALCPA and FASTER acts:

  • Milk
  • Eggs
  • Fish
  • Crustacean shellfish
  • Tree nuts
  • Peanuts
  • Wheat
  • Soybeans
  • Sesame

What the tags cannot tell you

  • What is in your specific products. Red curry paste frequently contains shrimp. Soy sauce usually contains wheat. Worcestershire sauce contains anchovy. Some brands of a given product contain an allergen and others do not.
  • Manufacturing cross-contact. May contain and processed in a facility with statements appear on packaging and only on packaging. We cannot know them.
  • What happened in your kitchen. Shared boards, shared oil, shared toasters and shared utensils all transfer allergens.
  • What happens when you substitute. Our substitution suggestions change the allergen profile, sometimes significantly.

If you or anyone you are cooking for has a food allergy, read every label yourself, every time, and treat our tags as a starting point rather than a clearance. Allergic reactions can be severe and rapid. If you are unsure about an ingredient, leave it out.

Food safety

We include food safety notes on recipes where they matter, sourced to guidance from the United States Department of Agriculture and the Food and Drug Administration. The essentials:

  • Cook to temperature, not to time. Poultry to 165°F (74°C). Ground meat to 160°F (71°C). Whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb and veal to 145°F (63°C) with a three-minute rest. Fish and shellfish to 145°F (63°C). Leftovers reheated to 165°F (74°C).
  • Raw and undercooked food carries risk. Raw or lightly cooked eggs, undercooked meat and poultry, raw shellfish, raw sprouts and unpasteurised dairy can carry bacteria. That risk is higher for pregnant people, young children, adults over 65 and anyone with a weakened immune system, who should avoid them.
  • Raw flour is not safe to eat. Flour is a raw agricultural product and has been linked to outbreaks. Do not taste raw dough or batter and do not let children eat it.
  • The two-hour rule. Perishable food should not sit between 40°F and 140°F (4°C and 60°C) for more than two hours, or one hour above 90°F (32°C).
  • Cool food fast. Shallow containers, into the refrigerator. A deep pot on the counter stays warm for hours.
  • Avoid cross-contamination. Separate boards for raw meat, and wash hands, knives and surfaces with hot soapy water after handling it.

Full storage times and temperature charts are on the kitchen basics page. For the authoritative source, see the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service and FoodSafety.gov.

If you spot a problem

If you think an allergen tag is wrong, a nutrition estimate is badly off, or a food safety note is missing where it should be, please tell us straight away. We treat those reports as urgent and we would much rather be corrected than be right.
