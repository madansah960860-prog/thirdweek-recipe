Sheet-Pan Lemon Chicken with Fennel and Olives

One half sheet pan, 40 minutes at 425°F, and a tray of chicken that browns instead of steaming because the vegetables under it are spread in a single layer.

Developed and retested in the Saltgrain test kitchen by Priya Raghunathan Published 08/14/2026 Last tested 08/29/2026
Prep time
15 minutes
Cook time
40 minutes
Total time
55 minutes
Yield
4 servings
Difficulty
Easy
Course
Dinner

This is the file we open when the week has gone sideways. Chicken thighs, potatoes and fennel go onto one half sheet pan, the oven does the rest, and what comes out is a tray of browned skin sitting in juices that read as lemon, oregano and olive brine at once. At Portland grocery prices it costs under fifteen dollars to put four servings on the table, and there is one pan in the sink.

Two variables decide the outcome. The first is the rub: garlic, lemon zest and oregano worked directly onto the meat and, where you can manage it, under the skin. The second is oven temperature. At 425°F (220°C) the potatoes build a crust and the fennel turns sweet at the edges. Anything cooler and the pan steams instead of roasting, which was the failure mode we hit most often in testing. We took it apart at length in the sheet-pan method.

Fennel is the ingredient people skip. Raw, it tastes assertively of anise. Roasted for forty minutes it reads closer to sweet onion, with the licorice note far in the background. Eight of the twelve people who ate the final round could not name it when asked. If you still want it out, thick onion wedges do the same structural job.

How we tested this

Four rounds, all on an 18 by 13 inch half sheet pan, with a thermometer sitting on the rack because the dial and the cavity rarely agree. Round one ran on the middle rack and the skin stayed pale, so round two moved to the upper third. Round three cut the fennel down to half-inch wedges, which is what finally got the fennel and the potatoes to finish at the same time. Round four held the olives back until the 25-minute mark, because olives that sit through the whole roast go leathery and push the whole pan salty. Round four is the version written below. It went to twelve tasters.

Method

  1. Heat the oven properly

    Set a rack in the upper third of the oven and heat it to 425°F (220°C). If you are using convection, drop it to 400°F (200°C) and start checking ten minutes early. Give the oven a full fifteen minutes after the beep; most home ovens lie about being ready.

  2. Rub the chicken

    Pat the thighs dry with paper towels, then dry them again. Mix the garlic, lemon zest, oregano, red pepper flakes and salt into a paste with a splash of the olive oil. Loosen the skin with a finger and push about half the paste underneath, then rub the rest over the top. Wash your hands and the board before you touch anything else.

  3. Dress the vegetables

    Toss the halved potatoes and fennel wedges on a half sheet pan (18 by 13 inches) with the remaining olive oil, the salt and the pepper. Spread them into a single layer with visible pan showing between pieces. Crowding is what makes vegetables steam.

  4. Add the chicken and start it

    Nestle the thighs skin side up among the vegetables. Tuck the lemon slices underneath a few of them so they do not scorch. Roast for 25 minutes without opening the door.

  5. Add the olives and finish

    Scatter the olives over the pan, give the potatoes a gentle turn, and return it for 12 to 15 minutes more. It is done when an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part of a thigh, away from bone, reads 165°F (74°C) and the skin is deep golden.

  6. Rest, then dress

    Let the pan sit for 5 minutes. Squeeze the lemon juice over everything, scatter the parsley and reserved fennel fronds, and drizzle the extra-virgin olive oil across the top. Serve straight from the pan, and spoon the juices over each plate.

Food safety

Cook chicken to an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) measured in the thickest part away from the bone, in line with USDA guidance. Wash hands, boards and knives with hot soapy water after handling raw poultry, and never rinse raw chicken in the sink, which spreads bacteria across your counters. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours.

Cook's tips

  • Salt the chicken the night before if you can. Rub it, set it on a rack over a plate, and leave it uncovered in the refrigerator overnight. The surface dries out, which is most of what crisp skin actually is. Twenty-four hours is the useful maximum.
  • Cut the potatoes to match the fennel, not the chicken. Halved baby potatoes and half-inch fennel wedges finish at the same moment. If your potatoes are larger than a golf ball, quarter them or they will still be chalky when the chicken is done.
  • Use the thermometer, not the clock. Thigh weight varies by more than you would think between packages, and a 40-minute recipe can genuinely need 48. Pull at 165°F (74°C) and stop guessing.
  • Do not wash the pan straight away. Tip the juices into a small bowl first. They are the best thing on the tray and there is never enough of them.

What goes wrong

  • Soft, pale skin. The thighs went in damp. Pat them dry, pat them dry again, and put half the rub under the skin rather than all of it on top.
  • Chalky potatoes beside finished chicken. The potatoes were too big. Halve baby potatoes and quarter anything larger than a golf ball so they match the half-inch fennel wedges.
  • Grey, steamed vegetables. The pan was crowded. Four servings fit one half sheet pan with bare metal showing between the pieces. Past six servings, use two pans.

Substitutions

  • No fennel: two medium yellow onions cut into thick wedges, plus ½ teaspoon fennel seed if you want the anise note back.
  • Boneless thighs: they work, but lose the skin and about eight minutes. Roast the vegetables alone for 15 minutes first, then add the chicken.
  • Bone-in breast: use two large split breasts, add them after the vegetables have had 10 minutes, and pull at the same 165°F (74°C).
  • Olives: any firm green olive works. Kalamata will bleed purple into the pan and taste sharper, which some people prefer. Rinse very salty olives first.
  • Potatoes: sweet potato chunks roast in the same window but go softer; add them fifteen minutes late if you want them to hold shape.

Make ahead, storage and reheating

  • Make ahead: the rub can be mixed up to three days ahead and kept covered in the refrigerator. Rubbed raw chicken keeps up to 24 hours before roasting.
  • Storage: cool leftovers quickly, then refrigerate in a shallow airtight container within two hours of cooking. USDA guidance for cooked poultry is three to four days at 40°F (4°C) or below.
  • Freezing: the chicken freezes well for two to three months. The potatoes do not. They go grainy on thawing, so pick them out before anything goes into the freezer.
  • Reheating: spread on a sheet pan, cover loosely with foil, and reheat at 375°F (190°C) for 12 to 15 minutes until the center reaches 165°F (74°C). Uncover for the last three minutes to bring the skin back.

What you need to cook it

A rimmed half sheet pan, an instant-read thermometer, and a sharp knife for the fennel. Nothing else. Our equipment guide explains what to look for in a sheet pan and why the cheap ones warp.

What to serve alongside

Bread. A torn loaf and a green salad with a sharp vinaigrette is the whole meal, and the bread is there to take the pan juices. If you want more on the table, our Italian at home collection has a panzanella that uses the same parsley and lemon.
